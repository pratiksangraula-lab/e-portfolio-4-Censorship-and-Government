## Artefact 1: Video from ABC News concerning under-16 ban in Australia

ABC News 2025, video, 10 December 2025. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dM22XaG1ifo

![Artefact 1 video](images/artefact1-screenshot.png)

### Description of the artefact

The video focuses on the first day of Australia's under-16 social media ban, during which hundreds of thousands of accounts of teenagers are disabled. Age verification is taking place while teenagers respond to their accounts being blocked the very next morning. Messaging, gaming and logged-out browsing remain possible (ABC News 2025). The legislation limits account ownership, not publication.

### Reason for choosing the artefact

A public broadcasting channel was selected instead of a commentary channel as the issue is controversial and the reporting was needed. The video relates to the case study presented during the workshop in regards to the same ban and its High Court review (Galea 2026, slide 29). What shocked me was how teenagers treated this as something done to them, not by them.

## Artefact 2: A news report that I have come across this week

Clover, J 2025, news report, Broadband TV News, 19 February 2025. [https://www.broadbandtvnews.com/2025/02/19/cloudflare-takes-legal-action-over-laligas-disproportionate-blocking-efforts/](https://www.broadbandtvnews.com/2025/02/19/cloudflare-takes-legal-action-over-laligas-disproportionate-blocking-efforts/)

![Artefact 2 article](images/artefact2-screenshot.png)

### Summary of the artefact

The Spanish court permitted the football league LaLiga to instruct providers to block the IP addresses hosting pirated matches broadcasts. Since the addresses were on a shared cloud network, some other websites went down with it and thus Cloudflare sued stating the blocking efforts as disproportionate (Clover 2025). In the later report, more than 13,500 websites are cited as affected (TechTimes 2026).

### Reasons why I have chosen the artefact

I have chosen this artefact because this is censorship without any government involvement: the private league chooses the block list while strangers suffer from it. This is similar to the ASIC case where a blocked IP address took down 250,000 websites (Galea 2026, slide 10). I had known how ineffective IP blocking could be but never expected this from a court.

## Artefact 3: Scholarly article

Shewly, HJ 2025, scholarly article, Dialogues on Digital Society, vol. 1, no. 3, pp. 457-462. DOI: 10.1177/29768640251381894

![Artefact 3 article](images/artefact3-screenshot.png)

### Summary of the artefact

In analyzing the 2024 blackout in Bangladesh, Shewly contends that network cuts constitute governance by absence as the government disconnects the infrastructure upon which protests are conducted and hinders coordination and hides the violence against the protesters. The author cites 296 blackouts in 54 countries in 2024 (Shewly 2025, p. 457).

### Reason for choosing the artefact

This was discovered from Google Scholar using the search term "internet shutdown protest 2025." The source describes the mechanism of blackouts and how they are carried out. The point I had not considered is the destruction of records through the blackout process.

## Artefact 4: Personal Reflection for the Workshop

Workshop: Week 9: Censorship and Government. Tutor: G Galea. Date: [EDIT: workshop date]. Campus: CQUniversity Sydney.

### Reflection of the artefact: personal reflection

My favorite concept from this was that of the chokepoint. There are no more post deletions now. People disable the layer upon which the posts travel, such as DNS, an ISP, or a registration rule. It became clear to me in Nepal (Galea 2026, slide 28). Young people posted on politicians' children showing their cars and vacations. Few days after that the government blocked 26 websites, and 19 people died while protesting.

### Rationale on why I chose the artefact

I chose my own reflection because nobody else can write it. I am in agreement with the protesters. The registration rule was months old, but used when the posts of corruption reached peak, thus the timing appears suspiciously convenient. I accept the contradiction. Those posts were posted by TikTok, which remained active because it was registered. The motive is questioned, not proved. That's how I learned about it from Spain. No corruption took place there, yet crude blocking disconnected thousands of harmless websites. The chokepoint controller does not have to compensate for being mistaken, and someday it might be me.
